The Problem

Why operational numbers are always wrong

Most growing businesses run on a patchwork of disconnected systems — a sales tool here, a spreadsheet there, a bookkeeper stitching it together once a month. Data lives in silos, month-end takes days, and margin is always out of date.

✕What it looks like today
  • Sales data in one system, inventory in another, accounting in a third
  • Supplier invoices post to cost of sales before the goods even arrive — margin is wrong from day one
  • Stock counts done on paper or in Excel, variances "adjusted" without anyone knowing why
  • Manual Xero journal entries every month — the same errors, every single time
  • No live view of what each branch, site, or product line actually costs versus earns
  • Clock-in and clock-out recorded on paper, re-typed into payroll
  • Products and assemblies aren't costed properly — nobody knows true margin per item
  • Customer credit accounts and vouchers tracked in a spreadsheet, if at all
  • Month-end takes three days of reconciliation instead of thirty minutes
  • Head office and branches run as if they were separate businesses
✓What CoreOps delivers
  • Sales, inventory, procurement, production, and accounting in one system
  • Cost of sales is recognised at the point of sale — never at the invoice date — so margin is always accurate
  • Digital stocktake with automatic variance calculation and one-click posting
  • Every transaction posts directly to Xero automatically — no CSVs, no re-typing
  • Live profit and loss by location, by category, by day
  • PIN-based clock in/out with automatic payroll export
  • Standard costing and material variance reporting down to the product level
  • Built-in customer accounts, credit, and voucher ledger — always reconciled
  • Month-end becomes: review, approve, done — every journal already coded correctly
  • Every site runs on one platform, with head office visibility over all of them

Financial Accuracy

Why margin is always wrong in disconnected systems

This is the most common and most costly accounting error in growing businesses. CoreOps eliminates it by design, using the same principle as a perpetual inventory ledger.

The Perpetual Inventory Principle

In a standard accounting workflow, the supplier invoice date drives when cost of sales hits the P&L. If a supplier invoices on the 28th but those goods are only sold or consumed the following month, one month's margin is understated and the next is overstated — every single cycle.

CoreOps uses a perpetual inventory ledger instead. Every stock event creates an immutable movement entry. Cost of sales is only recognised at the moment of sale or production consumption — never at receipt.

Step 1 — Goods received (approved)
DR Stock on Hand (Balance Sheet) CR Accounts Payable (Balance Sheet) → Cost of sales is NOT touched yet
Stock lands on the balance sheet. The P&L is untouched.
Step 2 — Item sold or consumed
DR Cost of Sales (P&L — this moment) CR Stock on Hand (Balance Sheet) → Cost of sales recognised at sale only
Margin is accurate to the day, every day.
